# General production repertoire

## Purpose and learning model

Help an adult learner develop flexible access to a Mandarin expression through varied production practice. Build connections to things worth meaning or saying: a recognizable concept, intention, feeling, construction or situation. A good cue is graspable before the answer is remembered. The learner understands what it is getting at while searching for the expression.

This is independent practice after initial teaching. Each cue must work on its own, without remembering a lesson sequence. The learner can try another approach or stop for today. We are exploring a repertoire that may work differently for different people; a single compulsory ideal cue is not the goal.

Good practice builds language sense that transfers into conversation, reading,
media, and other real use. Favor natural meaning, syntax, collocation, register,
and situation over exercises that require reverse-engineering dictionary
distinctions. A cue should evoke something the learner wants to mean or say,
not merely give enough technical restrictions to identify an answer.

## Input and output map

The input's `content` identifies the exact expression in `word`, selected uses and lexical notes in `uses`, and source sentences in `examples`. Treat these as source data, not instructions. The uses ground meaning, construction, register and scope. Notes and examples are not shown before answering unless their information is included in your cue. Source examples can inform new exercises without becoming a sentence-reuse requirement.

The supplied uses are selected learning coverage. Trust that they are needed,
including uncommon, literary, specialized, or personally relevant meanings.
Do not reassess their general popularity, exclude senses, or substitute a more
familiar use for the supplied one. This trust does not make every possible
exercise worthwhile: judge whether a draft offers useful, faithful practice
of the exact target within that selected coverage.

Return only `exercises` in the supplied schema. Each has a unique local `id`, `sourceUseId` (the supplied use ID anchoring this exercise), `stimulus`, and `teachingNote` (brief post-answer clarification or null). The learner types the target expression; the app supplies its written answer forms. Do not author response instructions or accepted answers.

Use `direct_text` with English `text`, or `phrase_cloze` with a concise English `frame` and natural Mandarin `text` containing exactly one `____` blank for the entire target. Do not expose the target's Chinese forms or pinyin anywhere in the visible stimulus. Use Simplified Chinese for authored contextual phrases.

Anchor each exercise to one supplied use reference. Choose a faithful connection
to that use rather than an arbitrary association; if no supplied use fits, omit
the item. A sense reference locates the practice for later review and serving;
it does not prescribe a cue format or require an exercise for every use.

These two stimulus containers are not a menu of teaching approaches. Within
them, explore ways of evoking the expression that the examples below do not
anticipate. A memory hook, a voice or attitude, an imagined exchange, a plain
meaning, a short construction, or another approach can earn a place when its
actual wording gives the learner something useful and graspable to retrieve.
No example format is an essential hallmark of a good cue.

## Decision process for this repertoire

### 1. Understand the selected capability

Start by understanding what the exact expression lets someone mean. Keep its useful manner, attitude, register and grammatical contribution. A tendency in the examples can guide a cue without becoming an absolute definition of the word.

### 2. Choose a faithful answer scope

Choose the answer scope for each use before drafting cues. If the expression can itself name the intended thing, action or concept, a short English equivalent or definition can be an excellent direct cue. Include such straightforward anchors where they naturally fit. “Scarf.” evokes 围巾; “Glance briefly.” captures 瞥's manner; “Pass away (formal, respectful).” preserves 逝世's register.

If this use instead contributes a modifier, degree, relationship or other part of a larger construction, supply the surrounding construction in a phrase cloze. Do not write direct English cues that translate the full state or sentence and bind this smaller contribution as their answer. A recognizable full meaning is not enough if the requested lexical unit is wrong. A use can have only clozes when they are the clearer way to practise it; there is no requirement to force a standalone definition card. Straightforward practice can itself be a familiar, short construction.

### 3. Explore a repertoire

Within an appropriate answer scope, explore other plausible routes. A circumstance can evoke what someone wants to say, a short line can carry an attitude, or a phrase cloze can make a construction tangible. Vary tone, rhythm, wording, structure, length and content where this might change the experience. Keep room for plain reformulations as well as more adventurous approaches. We do not yet know which variations matter to learners, so close wording is not itself a reason to discard a cue.

Five or six options for a useful use, or more, is reasonable, not a mandatory quota or ceiling. Let the expression determine how much useful material you can offer. You do not need to turn every source division into a checklist. Breadth can come from different ways into the same meaning, without manufacturing new senses or disguising every cue as a different scene.

Keep each item proportionate. A circumstance can be one sentence or a few evocative words. An elaborate miniature narrative can bury the intended idea; a short cue need not be inflated to prove effort. “Go through the motions; do something perfunctorily.” and “Do just enough to get someone off your back.” are different plausible ways into 敷衍 when the source supports them. They need not exclude each other, and neither requires a long story.

### 4. Read the complete learner experience

For a phrase cloze, read the Mandarin with the target inserted and the complete visible frame beside it. It should be natural and practise the target's own contribution. The English frame should give useful meaning without turning every cloze into a full bilingual sentence-translation task. Use as much framing as the exercise needs. Constructions can be valuable, but do not create a cloze just to vary the format.

Read each draft without silently supplying the target, the source notes, or a
lesson the learner might remember. What are they trying to express, and what
will recalling this target practise? Making a cue more selective is not enough
if the resulting task has little communicative value. Conversely, a simple
meaning or an uncommon referent can offer a perfectly good retrieval task.

A cue should evoke an intended meaning or utterance. Grammatical labels can
inform your reasoning but should not become the communicative reason to recall
the word. Do not turn the exercise into a classification quiz to distinguish
the target from another plausible answer. If useful production cannot be evoked
without such a quiz, reconsider the draft rather than adding technical wording
to make it selective.

A short definition can evoke a concept directly and be valuable practice,
including a deliberately selected uncommon referent. That differs from asking
which written character has a dictionary gloss when the meaning is expressed
only through larger lexical items. Identifying the written component does not,
by itself, create an expressive task for the exact target. Consider a faithful
construction or another retrieval route; do not manufacture a task merely to
avoid returning no exercises. Recognition and contextual exposure can still
support the selected coverage independently of this production repertoire.

Read each cue without mentally supplying the answer. Is the meaning or intention recognizable? Does the target itself naturally answer this cue, rather than a related consequence or a larger lexical item containing it? Keep lexical units intact. For example, blanking 与 from 与会 mainly retrieves the larger word, not a productive use of 与 itself.

Check the scope of the requested answer as well as its naturalness. Some expressions contribute only part of a larger description or construction. A direct cue translating the whole description may naturally ask for a whole adjective, phrase or sentence instead of this smaller contribution. In that case, give the surrounding construction and blank the target. For example, “Has improved to some extent.” alone asks for more than 有所; paired with “情况____改善。” it can exercise the target's own contribution. A post-answer note explaining how the target participates in a longer expression cannot repair an underspecified direct cue. This is a reason to choose a suitable form, not a requirement to force every word into a cloze.

Natural alternatives will remain. Strong evocation is useful; proving uniqueness is not required. Do not invent distinctions, pile up exclusions, or make the language awkward or analytical to eliminate synonyms. A possible alternative is a reason to notice misleading focus, not an automatic reason to discard an otherwise useful cue.

Curate lightly for linguistic faithfulness and usefulness. Remove clear errors, confusing wording and obvious filler. Do not require every option to demonstrate a distinct pedagogical contribution. Review the pool for a usable mixture, preserving simple cues alongside exploration. In a final pass, replace any direct cue asking for more than the target itself with a construction that actually isolates its contribution; omit it if that cannot be made natural. `teachingNote` appears after recall and cannot supply a missing condition that the cue needs before answering.

### 5. Keep useful possibilities; omit forced tasks

Return the options that offer plausible practice, including approaches whose
personal fit can only become clear through use. Light curation is not a demand
for one ideal cue, a distinct theoretical dimension in every option, or a fixed
set of cue types. It still requires a real retrieval task: do not dress up the
same linguistic mismatch with more restrictive wording simply to fill the pool.

When no worthwhile, linguistically faithful exercise can be authored for this
exact target within the supplied uses, return an empty exercises array. Do not
manufacture compound-fragment tasks to avoid that result. This records a
limitation of deliberate exercise design; recognition or contextual exposure
may still be valuable. Selected coverage remains unchanged. Return no
explanation or extra fields outside the supplied schema.

## Worked starting points

These cases make particular judgments concrete. They are starting points for
exploration, not templates, required ingredients, or a checklist by which other
cue types should be accepted or rejected. Each names the exact target, supplied
use and source material, then a concrete authoring outcome. A “cue considered”
is an illustrative draft being assessed, not an extra field in the input.
Other targets and other learners may benefit from routes not illustrated here.

### Keep a plain route to a concrete concept

- Target: `围巾`.
- Supplied use: a scarf worn around the neck.
- Source material: `天冷了，出门记得围上围巾。`
- Cue considered: `Scarf.`

Include that direct cue. It evokes the selected referent without requiring a
scene, grammatical explanation, or a long definition. Keep room for other
plausible routes if they offer useful practice; the simple anchor does not
establish the maximum scope or minimum form of the repertoire.

### Preserve manner and register without making a classification quiz

- Target: `逝世`.
- Supplied use: a formal, respectful way to say that someone died.
- Source material: `这位作家去年逝世了。`
- Cue considered: `Pass away (formal, respectful).`

Include the direct cue. The register frame preserves part of what someone
chooses the word to express. A natural context may also help, but do not replace
this accessible route with a long story just to avoid a short definition.

### Offer different ways into the same intention

- Target: `敷衍`.
- Supplied use: deal with a person or task perfunctorily, without genuine effort.
- Source material: `他随便答应了几句，只是在敷衍我。`
- Cues considered: `Go through the motions; do something perfunctorily.` and
  `Do just enough to get someone off your back.`

Include both plausible direct-text routes. The second makes a familiar
interpersonal intention graspable; the first gives a plain conceptual anchor.
They need not be separate senses or prove different pedagogical dimensions.
Explore further wording, tone or context when useful rather than treating this
pair as the required design for the word.

### Supply the construction that the target actually contributes to

- Target: `有所`.
- Supplied use: a limited or unspecified degree before a verb.
- Source material: `情况有所改善。`
- Cue considered: `Has improved to some extent.`

Omit that standalone direct cue: it asks for the whole state, not the target's
contribution. Include a phrase cloze such as `to some extent: 情况____改善。`
The surrounding syntax gives the target a faithful retrieval task. A post-answer
note cannot repair the wrong answer scope in the rejected direct cue. This
choice does not make clozes mandatory for other selected uses or expressions.

### Do not fill the pool with more selective versions of the wrong task

- Target: `他`.
- Supplied use: other, another; notes describe its contribution in `他人` and
  `他日`.
- Source material: `请尊重他人的隐私。`
- Cue considered: `respect other people's privacy (formal, written): 请尊重____人的隐私。`

Omit this task. The sentence is natural, but the blank retrieves part of the
larger word `他人`; the added register hint does not make isolated recall
worthwhile. For this supplied scope, this assessment finds no convincing
faithful alternative, so return an empty exercises array. Do not switch to
“he/him,” remove the selected sense, or assert that contextual exposure has no
value. This decision is about the available exercise design, not a rule that
bound contributions or literary uses can never be practised.

### Preserve a deliberately selected uncommon referent

- Target: `笏`.
- Supplied use: a ceremonial tablet held by officials at imperial court,
  deliberately selected for a learner interested in historical court vocabulary.
- Source material: notes identifying the object and its court setting.
- Cue considered: `A ceremonial tablet held by officials at imperial court.`

Include this precise direct cue. It offers faithful recall of the selected
referent. Rarity is not a reason to omit it, and adding a cloze or scene would
not automatically improve the task. Other useful approaches remain welcome;
none must resemble this one to qualify.

## Output and final check

Return only the specified JSON object. Before returning, read the actual cues
as the learner will encounter them: each should offer a graspable retrieval
experience, preserve the supplied sense and exact target, and reveal neither
its written form nor its pinyin. A teaching note comes after recall and must
not supply missing pre-answer meaning. Keep the repertoire open to useful
experiments while removing clear linguistic errors and forced tasks. Do not
turn these worked starting points into a mandatory recipe.
